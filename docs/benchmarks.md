# commiv benchmarks — full campaign, 2026-07-14

Every number in this file comes from one machine and one campaign (2026-07-13/14).
Raw per-run CSVs and the driver live in `~/cbench/campaign-2026-07-13/` (zenbook)
and `~/campaign/` (winserver); each family lists its repro command.

## Methodology

- **Machine**: AMD Ryzen 5 2600X (12 threads), 16 GB, WSL2 Ubuntu 24.04 on Windows 10.
- **commiv**: built `-Doptimize=ReleaseFast` (zig 0.16), 10 threads where the engine
  supports it, 3 seeds (1, 2, 3) unless stated.
- **Competitor walls are anchored**: commiv runs first with its published config; every
  competitor then gets commiv's measured wall (mean over seeds, rounded up, min 3 s) on
  the same instance. Nobody gets less time than commiv used.
- **Competitors**: LKH-3.0.14 (single-threaded, 3 seeds concurrently),
  HGS-CVRP (Vidal, single-threaded, 3 seeds concurrently), PyVRP 0.13 (single-threaded,
  3 seeds concurrently), VROOM 1.14 (10 threads, deterministic, one run).
  OR-Tools is excluded: measured non-competitive on every cell in the 2026-06 round.
- **Scoring**: road instances are scored by an independent validator on the true
  directed matrix; Solomon/GH PyVRP routes are re-scored with double-precision euclid
  (SINTEF convention). No solver's self-reported cost is trusted where a validator exists.
- PDPTW walls are 10/15/30/45/60/90 s by size, seeds 1-3 at n=100 and seed 1 for
  n>=200 (VROOM is deterministic; single-seed matches the earlier sweep convention).

## Money objective (PDPTW) — dollars burned, commiv vs VROOM

Same Li & Lim instances and walls, but the objective is money:
**cost = $140/vehicle + $0.50/unit distance + $0.50/unit route duration** (1 unit =
1 minute at ~40 km/h: $0.75/km x 0.667 km/min = $0.50/min driving; $30/hr driver =
$0.50/min of route duration incl. service and waiting). commiv optimizes this
natively (`PB_TIMEPEN=1 PB_VEH_PEN=280000`, single thread, seed 1). VROOM gets the
same $140 fixed cost per vehicle on a free fleet — the best it can express: it has
no way to price duration or waiting (VROOM-Project/vroom#1130). Both single-threaded,
equal wall. Money compared only where VROOM delivers everything.

**Read this table as a reference measurement, not a real-world win.** The dollar
figures rest on a declared unit mapping over synthetic academic instances, and the
competitor is scored on an objective it was never built to express. What it measures
honestly: the cost of not being able to price driver time, under stated prices, on a
standard instance set. Real-world claims need real matrices, real prices, and real
order flows.
